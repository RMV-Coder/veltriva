Veltriva is a local-first Windows browser built on Chromium (through Electron). It is for people who keep 50 or 100 tabs open because closing one feels like losing it. Veltriva unloads the tabs you left to free memory, moves the ones you stopped using out of the way, and remembers every page you visit, so a few partial words bring any of them back. Everything it remembers stays on your computer.

This is the first public release. v0.1.0 was prepared but never published.

## Install, update and remove

- **Install:** run the installer. It is one click, per user, and needs no administrator rights. It installs into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\veltriva`, adds Start-menu and desktop shortcuts named "Veltriva Browser", and starts Veltriva.
- **First start:** a welcome page asks which search engine to use, how much Veltriva should tidy for you, and whether automation should show its moves before making them. All three can be changed later in Settings.
- **Your data** lives in `%APPDATA%\Veltriva`: tabs, history, settings, site data and the log file.
- **Update:** run a newer installer. It replaces the app and keeps your data. Veltriva never updates itself.
- **Remove:** Settings ▸ Apps ▸ Installed apps ▸ Veltriva Browser ▸ Uninstall. After confirming, it asks whether to remove your Veltriva data too. **No is the default** and keeps it for a later install. A silent uninstall (`/S`) keeps your data as well; add `--delete-app-data` to remove it.

## What's in it

### Tabs that look after themselves

- **Unloading:** a tab you left hidden for an hour is unloaded to free its memory. It stays in the sidebar and comes back when you select it, with its back and forward history, where you had scrolled, and, until you quit, what you typed in plain fields.
- **Sleeping, then the Archive:** past your 10 most recent unpinned tabs, one you haven't used for 2 hours moves to **Sleeping**, and after 3 days there to the **Archive**. One click brings it back. The time counts your use of the computer, so a weekend away changes nothing.
- **Presets:** Gentle, Balanced (the default) and Aggressive set how fast this happens, and Settings shows every value behind them.
- **Low memory:** when Windows runs short, the tabs left longest are unloaded first. The tab you are using is never touched.
- **Your work is left alone:** a tab playing sound or video, using the camera or microphone, in fullscreen, with DevTools or a sign-in window open, with unsaved changes, or one you protected, is never moved or unloaded.
- **You stay in charge:** for your first 3 days, **Preview** shows what automation would move before it moves anything. Every move has **Undo**, **Activity** says which rule moved what, and **Pause automation** stops all of it.

### A memory you can search

- **Every page you visit is remembered** in your profile only, with its title and icon. Sign-in callbacks, addresses with a password in them and parameters that carry a token are left out.
- **Ctrl+Shift+K** opens the memory palette: a few partial words of a title, an address or a site find the page, whether it's open, Sleeping, archived or long closed, and Enter brings it back.
- **The address bar suggests** pages from your memory as you type. Nothing you type leaves your machine until you press Enter.

### Your session survives

- **Your tabs come back** after a restart or a crash, in their order, each with its history; only the selected one loads at once. The window opens where it was.
- **After two crashes in a row** right after starting, Veltriva opens your tabs without loading them (safe mode).
- **The database looks after itself:** it's checked after a crash, restored from a daily backup if it's damaged, and quitting never waits more than 3 seconds for it.
- **Ctrl+Shift+T** brings back a closed tab for 7 days, even after a restart.

### An everyday browser

Find in page, zoom per site, print, downloads with safe names (risky types stay unrunnable until you choose Keep), PDFs, DevTools, fullscreen with a notice naming the site, context menus, and the keyboard shortcuts you know. Permission requests name the site and are remembered; pop-ups open only after a click; links to other apps ask first; a certificate error has no way past it.

### Keyboard and screen readers

Everything works from the keyboard: F6 moves between the address bar, the sidebar and the page, and the sidebar and the palette have their own arrow keys. Screen readers hear how many suggestions and results there are and when an Undo or a prompt appears. The interface is checked against WCAG 2.2 AA in the light and dark themes.

### Diagnostics that stay on your machine

`veltriva://diagnostics` shows each tab's memory, what automation did and the log. **Export diagnostics** saves it for a problem report without page titles, and without addresses unless you tick the box. `veltriva://about` shows the versions and licenses.

### Measured

- **Unloading frees about 34 MB per tab:** with 100 tabs of a test page, 3,771 MB all live against 383 MB with 99 unloaded (median of 5 runs). Moving tabs to Sleeping without unloading them frees nothing. ([docs/benchmarks/memory.md](../benchmarks/memory.md))
- **Search answers in 10.3 ms** at the 95th percentile with 50,000 pages remembered. ([docs/benchmarks/search.md](../benchmarks/search.md))

Both ran on a Core i3 laptop with 7.9 GB under everyday load; the reports say how loaded it was.

## Privacy

- **No telemetry, no crash reports, no account, no cloud, no sync and no remote search suggestions.** Your pages, tabs, history and settings are stored in your profile and nowhere else.
- **The only traffic Veltriva makes for itself:**
  - the pages you visit;
  - the search engine you chose, when you press Enter on a search;
  - the icons of the sites you visit;
  - the update check, only if you turn it on.
- **Icon requests can't reach your own network.** Each one is checked first, and so is every redirect, so a web page can't use Veltriva to reach your router or your machine. One narrow gap remains (see the known limitations).
- **The update check is off by default.** While it's off, Veltriva makes no request, no DNS lookup and runs no timer for it. When on, it asks GitHub once a day and only shows a notice; nothing is downloaded.
- **You decide what's kept:**
  - **Pause memory** stops recording without a private window;
  - **site rules** keep a site out of the memory or away from automation;
  - **Clear data** really deletes: the database file is rewritten;
  - **Forget a site** removes its pages, visits, tabs and, if you like, its cookies and permissions;
  - pages are kept for 365 days unless you choose otherwise.
- **The log names tab ids and error codes, not what you browsed,** unless you turn on Diagnostic logging.
- **Spell checking** uses Windows' own spellchecker; nothing is downloaded for it.

## Known limitations

- **A page's `alert` and `confirm` boxes block the whole window** until you answer them, even from a background tab, and don't name the site. A page's `prompt()` shows nothing. Electron has no way to make them per tab yet.
- **A link inside a page that asks you to stay doesn't ask you:** the page keeps you. Typing an address, reloading, going back or forward and closing the tab do ask "Leave page?".
- **No Safe Browsing** protection against phishing or malware, and **no DRM video** (Widevine), so some streaming services won't play.
- **The database isn't encrypted at rest**, like mainstream browsers' history. Your Windows account and BitLocker protect it; another program running as you can read it.
- **Unsaved-work detection is a heuristic.** Veltriva keeps a tab you typed in, or one that asks before leaving, but a page that keeps work some other way may lose it when unloaded.
- **A muted video inside an embedded frame from another site isn't seen**, so its tab can be unloaded. With its sound on, the tab is kept.
- **Site icons are decoded by Veltriva's sandboxed interface:** only raster images up to 64 KB, checked by their bytes, but not re-encoded.
- **DNS rebinding can still steer an icon request:** Veltriva checks every address and redirect first, but a name whose answer changes between that check and the request isn't caught.
- **Unsigned builds:** SmartScreen warns about each new version until it builds a reputation.
- **Not in this version:**
  - multiple windows, and dragging a tab out;
  - private windows;
  - sync, extensions, a password manager and autofill;
  - ad and tracker blocking;
  - bookmarks and a history page (search the memory instead);
  - macOS and Linux.
- **Chromium security fixes arrive with each Electron release,** a little after Chrome. The project's policy is to ship each Electron security release within 7 days of it.
